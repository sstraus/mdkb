# Jina recall efficiency report (2026-10-17)

## Decision question

Does the Jina reranker provide enough useful Italian recall for its memory and prompt-path cost? **Not decided for everyday use.** Controlled labelled evaluations show a gain, but the available live rows do not label relevant answers or measure daily-user benefit. Keep this question separate from the daemon's total-memory investigation (`daemon-memory-investigation-2026-10-17.md`).

## Mechanism and fixed cost

`src/mcp/recall_rerank.rs` scores at most five MiniLM candidates for an automatic prompt. It admits only entries above a language-specific score floor. If the pool is empty, no model is loaded. A loading, timeout, busy, or failed rerank falls back to the MiniLM result. The code enables Italian reranking by default and disables English reranking by default. The default Italian floor is `-1.05`; the mdkb repository has a local override of `-1.65`, so its results must not be mixed with the default-floor evaluation. The prompt hook defaults to a 1000 ms deadline and reserves 150 ms after reranking; the rerank phase itself is capped at 700 ms.

The pinned Jina-v2 multilingual int8 weights are 280 MB on disk. The existing controlled model benchmark reports about 0.95 GB of loaded RSS and a 0.45 s top-five rerank at eight cores (0.22 s at 32 cores; see `docs/recall-options-eval.md`, lighter rerankers). The daemon holds the loaded model process-wide after the first eligible prompt (`src/llm/rerank.rs::ENGINE`); there is no idle unload. A new isolated off/on/off test on this Mac measured 260,352 / 1,524,112 / 257,008 KiB of daemon RSS after the same small-store prompt set. The Jina-on difference from the first off run is 1,263,760 KiB (1.205 GiB). This includes the Jina path's ONNX arenas and allocator effects, not only weight bytes. It is **not** an additive measurement of the live daemon's footprint. The separate memory report leaves the rest of the live peak unattributed.

## Quality evidence: labelled data

The shipped MiniLM-top-five/Jina-int8 path at the default floor injected the expected entry for 21 of 44 held-out Italian positives and admitted 0 of 24 Italian negatives. MiniLM alone at its clean cosine gate recovered 0 of those 44 positives and admitted 0 of 24 negatives. This is evidence that Jina can recover otherwise missed relevant entries on that labelled set, not proof of precision 100%: 0 of 24 negatives permits a wide uncertainty interval (reported upper Wilson bound 0.14). The positive set was written after reading corpus chunks; vocabulary overlap may make recall optimistic. The English held-out result admitted 3 of 24 negatives and the English store was synthetic, so it does not justify enabling English reranking.

A separate 40-positive fit-set comparison of a proposed e5-small retriever did not show a significant improvement over the shipped MiniLM pool. The shipped injected-recall figure there was 12/40, and e5-small top-ten reached 15/40 with four gains and one loss (paired p = 0.38) while doubling reranker work. These are different datasets and protocols from the 44-positive held-out result; do not combine their denominators.

## Latency and live utility

The earlier Mac held-out run, under high host load, measured 153 reranked hooks: unbounded Jina-phase p50 972 ms and p95 1513 ms. With the then 700 ms deadline, 65 runs timed out, 165 were busy, and 9 finished among 256 hook observations; these groups are not all 153 reranked calls. A timeout or busy result falls back, so memory can stay resident even when Jina contributes no recall for that prompt. Those numbers were measured under contention and with an earlier hook-client wait; they are **not** today's latency.

A read-only sample of the orchestrator store's `user_prompt_submit` hook log, from the current daemon start at 20:42:40 CEST through 23:09:44, contained 62 rows: 6 `below_gate` reranks, 1 `loading`, 24 `off`, and 31 with no rerank outcome. The six completed reranks had a recorded rerank-phase median 190.5 ms, maximum 429 ms, and injected no context; the log has no relevance labels, so `below_gate` is not a false-negative verdict. The 24 `off` rows are not failed Jina runs. The mdkb repository's `-1.65` override is not used for this orchestrator sample. This live sample shows successful model execution but does not establish recall value per GB.

## Cross-repository telemetry survey (2026-10-18, read-only)

This survey reads only event/outcome/timing metadata from the exact repository roots in the daemon's `repos.json`; it does not read prompt text. Over the seven days before 10:42 CEST, 55 of 56 registered roots had a hook log and 35 had recent `user_prompt_submit` rows. The 1,948 rows included 17 `ok` Jina outcomes, 83 `below_gate`, 353 `not_cached`, 24 `timeout`, 6 `loading`, 4 `busy`, 4 `no_budget`, 123 `no_candidates`, 730 `off`, and 604 without a rerank outcome. These are **event counts, not relevance labels or unique user prompts**; a missing or rotated log is not evidence of no usage.

TUICCommander had 472 rows in that seven-day window: 289 `off`, 137 `not_cached`, 43 with no rerank outcome, and 3 `no_budget`. It had **no recorded `ok` or `below_gate` Jina scoring** in that window. In the most recent 24 hours it had 44 rows (26 `off`, 18 without a rerank outcome). The older `not_cached` rows may predate installation of the model weights; the recent rows do not establish that Jina was eligible for those prompts. The mdkb repository had 56 rows in the seven-day window, but none in the most recent 24 hours. Orchestrator had 541 rows, including 12 `ok` and 58 `below_gate`; it is not a substitute for the TUICCommander workload.

The recall ledger deliberately stores no prompt text (`src/store/recall_ledger.rs`, `src/store/schema.rs`), and the hook log stores outcomes and phase times only. Existing telemetry can measure **opportunity, execution and cost** per repository. It cannot tell whether an injected entry answered a real user question. A definitive multi-repository quality score needs a representative, labelled prompt/reference set. The operator subsequently authorized access to existing conversation records for this evaluation; the inventory below establishes availability, not relevance. No logging or live configuration change was required for the metadata survey.

## Existing conversation coverage and evaluation limits (2026-10-18)

The operator authorized inspection of existing conversations for this evaluation. TUICommander's `docs/FEATURES.md` says AI Chat conversations live in ego and terminal Chat reads the respective host transcript. The documented ego interface is `ego ls --all --output json` followed by `ego export ID --format jsonl`; the Codex rollout path is documented in TUICommander's `docs/FEATURES.md`. Both exports can contain private content. This inventory printed counts and structural metadata only, not prompt text, and did not modify a store.

A seven-day inventory of ego's session catalogue found 548 recently updated sessions with `tuicommander` in the workspace path, 28 with `orchestrator`, and 6 with `mdkb`. The analogous Codex rollout inventory (first `session_meta.payload.cwd`, file modification time) found 1,222, 2,315, and 284 files. These are *sessions/files*, not distinct users, prompt counts, eligible recall calls, or sessions definitely owned by the primary checkout. Worktree paths are included. A single file can have several turns. The counts cannot be joined to the mdkb hook rows: the hook telemetry has no prompt text, and these host inventories have no verified event-to-prompt mapping. Claude JSONL is another partial source, with only five recently modified TUICommander files in the earlier seven-day inspection; those contained 17 short candidate user-text messages, including automatic feedback and follow-ups. The different source windows and clocks preclude adding these counts together.

For a bounded feasibility check, the four most recently updated ego sessions in each of the three workspace groups were exported without printing their text. Their completed `user_message` counts were 30 (TUICommander), 9 (orchestrator), and 19 (mdkb). Three, three, and one respectively matched a narrow automatic-introduction/checkpoint prefix; 18, 5, and 5 passed only a crude 80–2,000-character and introduction-prefix screen. Nine mdkb messages were shorter than 40 characters. For Codex, the eight most recently modified rollout files per group contained 17, 16, and 16 user-role rows; only 1, 0, and 0 passed that same crude screen. **None of these screens verifies that a turn is a natural recall question or has an answerable indexed reference.** Automatic agent packets, large handoffs, short contextual follow-ups, duplicated host representations, and commands need manual review. Selection by most recent modification is biased toward active or long-running sessions; it is not a representative sample of hook events.

A valid next pilot would draw real *human* user turns across dates, repositories, hosts, and languages; exclude automatic agent packets and hook feedback; keep contextual follow-ups only when their prior turn can be reconstructed. A TUICommander conversation check exposed a critical time-leak trap: a genuine question about slow `make dev` was submitted on 2026-10-10 at 10:25 UTC, but the directly answering `mbx-disables-incremental-make-dev` memory entry was created on 2026-10-12 at 12:32 UTC. Scoring that question against today's store would count knowledge that did not exist when the question was asked. Restrict references to entries present at prompt time, or label the retrospective test separately as present-day usefulness rather than historical hook effectiveness. Before model scores are viewed, a reviewer must mark each question answerable or not against an authoritative memory/document version at the time of the prompt, record the relevant entry IDs and ambiguous or missing cases, and then freeze labels. For each labelled prompt, retrieve the same production MiniLM top-five pool and its baseline gate, score that pool with Jina, apply the actual language floor and hook deadline, and count relevant admissions and false admissions for both paths. Report coverage, exclusions, paired changes, and fallback outcomes by repository; do not treat an assistant's answer or a reranker score as the reference label. The existing `examples/recall_options_eval.rs` expects separate `corpus.json`, `it_set.json`, `sample.json`, and `memory-recall.json` fixtures, and does not consume live conversations or reproduce the complete hook deadline. A copy of the large TUICommander index or a new live daemon is not justified by this inventory.

**No independent reference labels or paired Jina/MiniLM scores were produced from these conversations.** Therefore the multi-repository quality effect and the value per GiB remain unmeasured, notwithstanding the newly confirmed availability of some conversation sources. In particular, TUICommander's recent hook rows did not execute a recorded completed Jina scoring path. Do not infer zero benefit or a positive benefit for TUICommander from those rows. This is an incomplete quality evaluation, not a failed relevance test.

## Efficiency assessment and next gate

Jina has demonstrated *potential* quality gain for Italian recall, at a measured marginal cost of about 1.2 GiB of daemon RSS in the isolated small-store test and a variable latency that has exceeded the hook budget under contention. Today's small live sample spent rerank time without admitting a hit, but it is not a labelled effectiveness study. Neither a memory-only decision to remove Jina nor a claim that it is cost-effective follows from these data.

For a decision, label the authorized existing conversation prompts against authoritative references before changing any floor. Record relevant hits and false admissions for each path under its predeclared production gate and a shared candidate pool, plus `loading`/`timeout`/`busy`/`ok`/`below_gate`, end-to-end hook latency, CPU, and daemon footprint for each isolated run. Specify acceptable memory, latency and false-admission limits before judging the trade-off. No live setting, model cache or live daemon was changed to prepare this report.
